# Telegram Writing Rules

## Purpose

This document defines the writing style for every Telegram post published by the project.

The goal is not to create advertisements.

The goal is to write recommendations that people enjoy reading and genuinely find useful.

Every post should feel like it was written by someone who discovered an interesting product and decided it was worth sharing.

Long-term trust is more important than maximizing clicks on a single post.

---

# The Reader

Assume the reader is quickly scrolling through Telegram.

Most readers decide within a few seconds whether a post is worth reading.

The opening should therefore communicate the product's main value immediately.

---

# Writing Philosophy

Every post should answer one simple question:

**"Why should someone care about this product?"**

Focus on the benefit before the product itself.

Do not simply describe features.

Explain why the product is useful.

Whenever possible, help the reader imagine themselves using the product.

---

# Reader Motivation

A successful post should make the reader feel one or more of the following:

- That solves a problem I have.
- I didn't know this existed.
- That would actually make life easier.
- That's surprisingly useful.
- That seems like a great value.
- That would make a nice gift.

---

# Sell The Benefit

Always prioritize the benefit over the technical specifications.

Instead of describing what the product is, explain why someone would want it.

The benefit should be obvious before mentioning detailed specifications.

---

# Product Explanation

Keep explanations simple.

The reader should immediately understand:

- What the product is.
- Why it is useful.
- When they would use it.

Avoid unnecessary technical details.

---

# Writing Style

The writing should feel:

- Natural.
- Friendly.
- Helpful.
- Honest.
- Conversational.
- Easy to read.

Avoid sounding like an advertisement.

Avoid sounding like a product catalog.

Avoid overly formal language.

---

# Variety

Avoid making every post feel identical.

Vary the opening style.

Possible opening approaches include:

- A common problem.
- A useful benefit.
- A surprising fact.
- A practical use case.
- A gift idea.
- A question.
- A relatable situation.

Posts should feel fresh rather than generated from the same template.

---

# Honesty

Never exaggerate.

Never invent product capabilities.

Never create fake urgency.

Never create fake scarcity.

Never make promises that cannot be verified.

Trust is always more valuable than a single click.

---

# Emojis

Emojis should improve readability.

Use them naturally.

Do not add emojis simply because a template expects them.

If removing an emoji makes no difference, it probably wasn't necessary.

---

# Structure

Most posts should naturally follow this flow:

1. Capture attention.
2. Explain the product.
3. Explain why it is useful.
4. Highlight the main benefits.
5. Present the price.
6. Invite the reader to learn more.

This structure is a guideline rather than a strict rule.

Clarity is always more important than consistency.

---

# Call To Action

The call to action should feel natural.

Invite curiosity rather than pressure the reader.

The goal is to encourage interest, not force a click.

---

# Final Principle

Every published post should leave the reader thinking:

> "I'm glad I saw this."

Never:

> "I was just advertised to."
